# Changelog

## 0.1.0 - 2026-10-12

- First version: the popup to save an offer, the board with a column per status, and the options (language, columns, export and import).
- The data is split in ten models (company, source, offer, application, status, contact, notes, timeline, reminders) and kept in `chrome.storage.local`, shared by the pages.
- English and French.
- Appearance: light, dark or the theme of the system, seven accent colours, two colour-blind modes (red-green, blue-yellow) that use the palette of Okabe and Ito and give each column a shape, a mode for people who see no colour, and high contrast. The choice applies to every page at once.
- Contrast and vision are two settings, so that both can be chosen. The contrast follows the system by default: more contrast asked, or the colours forced (the contrast themes of Windows), and it comes before a custom theme. When the system forces its colours, a column shows its shape in place of its colour and a message its sign. In the vision modes a message also gets a sign, since its colour may not be seen.
- The colours of the messages and of the columns were checked under a simulation of the three kinds of colour blindness: the text colours keep 4.5:1 against the page (the standard look included), the colours of the columns keep 3:1 against the light and the dark surface. A test guards them.
- Layout: a very long word or name breaks inside its card instead of going out of it, a long badge is a rounded box that wraps (not a bubble) and goes to the next line before squeezing the others, and the lines of the columns and of the tags in the options keep their small symbol field in a narrow column.
- Text spacing: the spacing of the letters is left out in Arabic and in the Indic and South-East Asian scripts, where it split the words and the syllables; the spacing of the words and the lines stays.
- Forced colours: a badge gets a line when the system takes its background away, so that it stays a badge and not a lost word.
- Keyboard and screen reader: a link that skips the header is the first stop of every page, and a message of error is tied to its field (`aria-describedby`, `aria-invalid`) as well as being said when it appears.
- Keys: `/` goes to the search of the board, the arrows move from card to card and column to column, and Shift with an arrow moves the card to the neighbour column and says so. A setting in the options turns the keys of the pages off. The browser can open the popup (Alt+Shift+J) and the board (Alt+Shift+L), and the user changes those keys in the browser.
- Time: every moment is stored in UTC and shown in the time zone chosen in the options (the one of the browser by default); a reminder is typed in that time zone. The day an offer was published is a plain date that no time zone can move. See `docs/formats.md`.
- Money: a salary is a range with its currency, its period and gross or net, kept in minor units and shown with the format of the currency and of the language (symbol, code or name). The salaries are compared per year in a home currency with the rates the user types. The columns can be renamed again (the name field did not save).
- Contacts: a phone number is kept in the international format (E.164) and shown as such, or in the national format; the email in lower case; the LinkedIn page as its canonical address; the country as an ISO code, shown by its name; the time zone and the languages of the person, with the time it is there. A phone typed without a country code is read in the country of the person or in the default country of the options.
- Contacts: a person can have several ways to be reached (emails, phone numbers, LinkedIn pages, websites, other), each with a label and one main way per type, in a new model. Making another way the main one, removing the main one (the oldest left takes its place) and adding the same value twice are handled.
- Exchanges: a new model for what was said with someone: an email, a LinkedIn message, a call, an SMS, a video call or a meeting, received or sent, with its contact, its moment in UTC, its subject and its text, and the length of a call. They are listed in the drawer and shown in the timeline with the rest of the history. An exchange that carries an external reference (the Message-ID of an email) is kept once.
- Importing exchanges: paste a text (an email with its headers is read as one), or choose email files (.eml), a JSON list or a CSV file (comma, semicolon or tab). A preview shows what was understood before anything is saved: the channel, the direction, the contact (found by the address, then by the name), the date. A row with a problem says which and cannot be chosen; a date that is missing can be typed. What was imported before is not added again. A new setting holds the addresses of the user, to tell a sent email from a received one.
- Classifying: tags, with a colour, on applications, companies, contacts and exchanges (two new models, a polymorphic link); a favourite star on applications, companies and contacts; a priority on applications (none, low, medium, high). A tag is added by its name, with the names that exist offered; two names that differ only by case or accents are one tag. The tags are managed in the options (rename, colour, remove, how many things carry each). The cards show the star, the priority and the tags.
- The board: a search that ignores case and accents and wants every word (in the title, the company, the place, the source, the tags and the people); filters by tag (all of them), favourites, source, company, lowest priority and late reminders; a sort by the order of the board, the last change, the date added, the salary per year in the home currency, the priority, the company or the job title, in the way of the language (accents, numbers), with what cannot be compared last. The view is kept, and the board says how many applications are shown.
- Reading a page: when the popup opens on a job page, it reads the `JobPosting` data the page publishes (title, company, place, contract, remote work, description, salary, day published) and fills the form. Only the page you opened the popup on is read, once, and nothing leaves the browser. The day published follows the time zone of the page when it gives one, and yours when it does not.
- Reading a page without `JobPosting` data: the OpenGraph tags and the title of the page give the title and the description. The name of the site is never taken for the company.
- Report a reading that broke: a link in the popup opens a prefilled GitHub issue with the site, the version and what was read. Nothing is sent by the extension.
- The same report can be opened as an e-mail, for someone with no GitHub account.
- LinkedIn: the popup reads an offer page of the French or English interface (title, company, place, day published from "3 days ago", salary of the offer, description), even though LinkedIn publishes no structured data. For this site only, the main part of the page is read, on the page you opened the popup on.
- Reading a page, checked on real pages of nine job sites: the periods `YEARLY`, `MONTHLY`, `DAILY`, gross or net read from the text of a salary, a yearly salary in thousands left out instead of guessed, no repeated city in the place, readable contracts, decoded HTML entities, dates written with a space before the offset.
- Backup to a file: one button writes everything and the settings to a readable JSON file named with the date. Where the browser allows it, the file is chosen once and overwritten at each backup; otherwise it is downloaded. The board reminds you when the last backup is older than the number of days you set (30 by default). Importing a backup brings the settings back too.
- Restore a backup: choosing a backup file shows what would change (per kind of data: in the backup, new, different, the same, only here) before anything is applied. It replaces the data, as a restore after a loss should; cancelling changes nothing.
- Versions of the data: the structure of the data now has a version of its own (2), migrations that bring older data and older backups up to date, and a refusal, with nothing overwritten, for data from a newer version. Every record has the moment of its last change, and a deleted record goes to a trash for 30 days, where the options can put it back.
- Fixed: the default sources shared their objects between stores.
- Backups carry a checksum. A restore tells whether the file is as it was saved, and asks for an explicit yes when it is not (a file edited by hand, cut off or damaged). Reformatting the file does not break it.
- Backups protected by a passphrase: a switch encrypts the backup in the browser (AES-GCM, key from PBKDF2-SHA256 with 600 000 iterations and a random salt) before it is written. The passphrase is typed twice, never kept, and asked again on the next page; a typing mistake is caught by a stored check. Restoring a protected backup asks for the passphrase, and a wrong one, or a file that was changed, fails without changing anything.
- Every change is written at once (at the end of the turn it was made in) instead of after a delay of a fraction of a second: a creation, a change, a deletion, a move, and the view of the board. Closing a tab right after an action can no longer lose it.
- Fixed: a change made right after a write could be overwritten when the browser told the page about its own write. A page now recognises the echo of its own write and leaves its data alone.
- Fixed: when two changes were written one after the other, the echo of the first could briefly put the data back as it was and, if the page changed it again at that moment, lose the second change. A page now keeps the last things it wrote and recognises all their echoes.
- Text that was typed and not saved: the form of an offer shows its unsaved changes and saves only when there are some; the drawer asks before it is closed with unsaved text, and the browser asks before the board or the options are; the popup keeps a draft of what is typed and gives it back on the same page.
- Reminders: once you have applied, the application suggests a follow-up reminder at 9 in the morning, some days later (7 by default, a setting), in your time zone. It is added only when you accept, and not offered for an archived or finished application, or one that already has a reminder waiting.
- Reminders that tell: the icon of the extension shows how many reminders are due and not done, and, if you turn it on, a notification of your system tells about a reminder when it comes due (once, in your language and time zone; a click opens the application; many at once make one; it stays on screen until you close it, which you can turn off). The notifications are an optional permission, asked only when you turn them on.
- Export the board as a CSV file for a spreadsheet: the applications that are shown, in the order shown, with their dates in UTC and their salary per year in the home currency. Semicolon and decimal comma in French, comma and decimal point otherwise; a text that would run as a formula stays text.
- Companies tied to one another: a recruitment firm and its final client, a subsidiary and its parent. Added from the drawer by the name of the other company; shown on the card (Hire Co → Big Bank), found by the search and in the CSV export. A link to itself, a twin link and a circle are refused. New version of the structure of the data (3), with its migration.
- People and their companies: a person can work for several companies, with a title and a period each, and help with several applications. From a contact you add a company, say they left it, and link them to another application; from the other application they are there too. New version of the structure of the data (4), with its migration. The persistence tests no longer depend on short fixed waits.
- Estimating the net pay: pay profiles (contributions and taxes as rates or scales, ordered, written by you for your country and status) with a few editable examples, the estimate of the net pay of an offer in its drawer with the calculation on request, and the net in the CSV. Always an estimate, never a promise; not made when it would be a guess. New version of the structure of the data (5).
- Freelance day rate: from the income you want, the day rate to ask (and the hourly one), or from a day rate, what is left. The charges come from a pay profile or a flat rate, applied to the turnover or to the profit. Found by search, so a profile with a scale works.
- Talent.com: the popup reads an offer page (title, company, place) from the title and the tags of the page, without reading the page itself.
- A short journal of what each page wrote to and read from the storage (counts and fingerprints, no data) is kept in memory, and printed by the browser tests when two pages disagree.
- The address of an offer: read from the page when it gives one, or typed; a link opens it in the map service you choose (Google Maps, OpenStreetMap, Apple Maps or your own link), with a route from your address; you keep what you found there (distance, time, how you travel) in the offer, on the card and in the CSV. The extension asks nothing of any service. New version of the structure of the data (6).
- Fixed a loss of data between pages: a page that had not yet seen a change made in another page (the board and the options, the popup) could write its older copy over it, and a priority or a time zone set in one page could disappear. A page now merges its changes into what the storage holds, record by record, and the settings are written one by one. The default columns and sources have fixed ids.
- Fixed: a setting typed letter by letter (the address you leave from, a link of your own) could lose its last letters, because the storage told the page about the older writes. Settings are written together and an older write is no longer read back over what is typed.
- The address in fields: lines 1 to 3, postal code, city, region, country and, if known, the position on a map; taken from the page when it gives them split, otherwise typed. The same fields for the address you leave from. With the position at both ends OpenStreetMap makes a route (by car, bicycle or on foot); there is none for public transport. The extension looks nothing up. New version of the structure of the data (7).
- Journeys to work in legs: on foot, bus, train, metro, suburban train, with the time of each leg and the total, a way there and a way back (the same legs in reverse), one of them preferred and shown on the card and in the CSV. Nothing is computed by the extension. New version of the structure of the data (8): what was kept as a distance and a time becomes a journey of one leg.
- Bringing two computers together: the data of another computer's backup is added to this one after a preview, without deleting anything; the most recent version of a record wins; the default columns and sources are matched, not doubled; the risk of duplicates is shown and must be accepted. Reading a backup held by the interface no longer fails on a real backup (a copy of reactive objects was refused).
- Text appearance: the font (system, serif, monospace, or one installed on the computer), the size from 80% to 200%, the spacing of the text (up to the widest of WCAG 1.4.12), the density of the interface and the reduction of movement. The font is always followed by the system fonts, so every script keeps its letters. No font is shipped with the extension.
- A symbol of your own for each column and each tag (a letter, a sign or an emoji), shown in front of the name and hidden from screen readers. One character is kept; a symbol the fonts cannot draw is replaced by the default shape. New version of the structure of the data (9).
- The page tells its language and direction (`lang`, `dir`) from the language chosen, from a table that describes each language (code, own name, direction, script). The list of languages names each one in itself.
- The pages use logical CSS (start, end, block, inline) and hold when the writing runs from right to left; a Stylelint rule forbids left, right, top and bottom from now on.
- Search and sort follow the language of the text: names are compared without minding the case, the accents or the form of Unicode (a company or a tag typed again is found, not doubled), names are stored in one form of Unicode (NFC), tags are sorted by the rules of the language, and a search in Chinese, Japanese or Thai is cut into words where the browser knows how.
- Texts that depend on a number follow the plural rules of the language (`Intl.PluralRules`: one, few, many, other…) and write the number with the digits of the language; the texts counted so far are the usage of a tag, the confirmation of its removal, the trash, the reminders notification, the import, the export and the merge.
- The name and the description of the extension are translated (`_locales`, English and French). A test checks that every language has the same placeholders as English, and a page describes how to add a language.
- A test stretches every text of the pages by 40% with accents, with taller lines and from right to left, and checks that nothing is cut or sticks out of the page (with a control that must be found).
- Your own themes: set the colours of the page (background, surface, text, accent, text on the accent, success, warning, error, borders), one set for the light look and one for the dark look, with a live preview, a warning when two colours are too close to read (WCAG 4.5 to 1 for text, 3 to 1 for lines) and a button that moves the colour as little as needed to correct it. Several themes with a name, exported and imported as a small file. A colour for each of eight columns, in each set (the ninth column uses the first colour again); with a theme in use they replace the colours of the columns, in every vision mode, and the shapes stay.
- Your own language: download a file with the texts, translate it, give it back. It is checked before anything is kept (size, shape, hidden control and direction characters, markup or links that run code, the placeholders of every text, plural forms of the language) and a text that does not pass stays in English. Up to five languages, kept on this computer, apart from the data and the backups.
- Dates: the format follows the language (13/10/2026 in French, 10/13/2026 in English…) and the user can override it: day/month/year, month/day/year, year-month-day, with the month name (short, long, with the weekday) or a format of their own (`dddd D MMMM YYYY`, text kept in square brackets), and the hour in 24 or 12 hours. Dates can also say how long ago ("3 days ago", "yesterday"), alone or with the date; a notification says how long the reminder has been due, with its date written in full.
- Languages: Spanish, German, Italian, Portuguese (Portugal), Dutch and Arabic (written from right to left), with the names of the store listing. They were translated without a person who speaks them reading the result: the options say so under the language list, with a link that opens a pre-filled issue on GitHub to report a mistake (nothing is sent by the extension). A language checked by a native speaker loses the notice. The CSV file of a language that writes numbers with a decimal comma uses semicolons.
- Languages: the columns and the source "Other" that come with the extension are shown in the language of the page until they are renamed (the stored name stays English). The error messages about a file or the data, the "Support the stream" link and the flat charges line are translated too.
- Languages of your own: the code can end in a private-use part (`es-x-mine`), which the browser reads as the language before it. A language added by a user whose code the extension later comes with is kept under `<code>-x-mine` instead of being hidden. A developer mode lets a language file replace a language of the extension on this computer only, to try a translation before sending it.
- Glassdoor: the offer that is open is read (title, company, place, pay range and description) from the attributes of the page that stay the same; a list of offers says nothing. Indeed and WeLoveDevs were already read from their JobPosting data.
- France Travail: the page of an offer is read: title, place, contract, the day it was published and the pay (annual, monthly, hourly…) from its tab and its description tag, and the employer and the text of the offer from the component that holds them in its own shadow DOM, which the snapshot now carries for the sites that have an adapter. The list of offers says nothing.
- Pay read from a page: gross or net and the period are taken from the words next to the same amount in the text ("40 k€ brut annuel"); a period nobody says is a year, unless the figure is clearly a month of pay in euros, pounds or francs (3000 €) or an hour (15 €). A region that is a code of the site ("A8") is left out of the place.
- Sites: a fixture of the structure of the page of an offer for eleven sites, with a command that compares a freshly saved page with it and says which field stopped being read (`docs/sites.md`).
- Accessibility: every field has a label its control is tied to (the library did not tie them), a message of error or confirmation is read out when it appears, the title of a card is a button for the keyboard, each column is a named region, targets are 24 pixels, and the pills of the footer have a readable contrast. A new suite scans the pages with axe-core in Chrome and Firefox, in both looks and the vision modes, and measures them at 1024, 1280 and 1920 pixels (`docs/accessibility.md`).
- Layout: the board takes the width of the window (the six columns are seen side by side from 1200 pixels), the options come in two columns from 1200 pixels, the drawer is wider on a wide window, the reminder to back up is one line, and the titles of the options have an even rhythm.
- Languages: each language but English is read from the extension only when it is used (a chunk of its own), instead of every page carrying all of them; adding a language is adding its file. The texts of the notification of the reminders are in a small file for each language (`src/locales/notify`), which is all the background reads (it went from 533 to 266 kB). In the developer mode, a button sends a test notification with every text and every plural form of the language in use, using the `notify` texts of the file that was added.
- Messages: what was done (saved, imported, restored, backed up, added) is told in a message over the page, above the drawer too, that goes after a few seconds, stops while the pointer or the focus is on it, and has a button to close it; an error stays until it is closed. They are read out by screen readers (polite for a success, assertive for an error). Errors of a field stay next to the field.
- Questions: the box of the browser (`window.confirm`) is replaced by a dialog of the extension (a native `<dialog>`), in the language and the look in use; it is used for what cannot be undone (emptying the trash, deleting all the data, closing with text that was not saved).
- Undo: deleting an application, a journey, a tag or a pay profile asks nothing: it goes to the trash and the message offers to put it back, with what went with it. The same for a theme and a language of your own, which are put back as they were.
- Languages: Hebrew (written from right to left), Japanese, Chinese in simplified and in traditional characters, Korean, Hindi, Polish, Russian, Ukrainian, Turkish and Swedish, with the names of the store listing. Like the others they were translated without a person who speaks them reading the result, and the options say so. The language of the browser is found by its script or its region (`zh-TW` and `zh-HK` are traditional Chinese, `zh-CN` and `zh-SG` simplified), then down to the language.

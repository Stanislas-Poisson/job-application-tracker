# Changelog

## 0.1.0 - 2026-10-12

- First version: the popup to save an offer, the board with a column per status, and the options (language, columns, export and import).
- The data is split in ten models (company, source, offer, application, status, contact, notes, timeline, reminders) and kept in `chrome.storage.local`, shared by the pages.
- English and French.
- Appearance: light, dark or the theme of the system, seven accent colours, two colour-blind modes (red-green, blue-yellow) that use the palette of Okabe and Ito and give each column a shape, and a high-contrast mode. The choice applies to every page at once.
- Time: every moment is stored in UTC and shown in the time zone chosen in the options (the one of the browser by default); a reminder is typed in that time zone. The day an offer was published is a plain date that no time zone can move. See `docs/formats.md`.
- Money: a salary is a range with its currency, its period and gross or net, kept in minor units and shown with the format of the currency and of the language (symbol, code or name). The salaries are compared per year in a home currency with the rates the user types. The columns can be renamed again (the name field did not save).
- Contacts: a phone number is kept in the international format (E.164) and shown as such, or in the national format; the email in lower case; the LinkedIn page as its canonical address; the country as an ISO code, shown by its name; the time zone and the languages of the person, with the time it is there. A phone typed without a country code is read in the country of the person or in the default country of the options.
- Contacts: a person can have several ways to be reached (emails, phone numbers, LinkedIn pages, websites, other), each with a label and one main way per type, in a new model. Making another way the main one, removing the main one (the oldest left takes its place) and adding the same value twice are handled.
- Exchanges: a new model for what was said with someone: an email, a LinkedIn message, a call, an SMS, a video call or a meeting, received or sent, with its contact, its moment in UTC, its subject and its text, and the length of a call. They are listed in the drawer and shown in the timeline with the rest of the history. An exchange that carries an external reference (the Message-ID of an email) is kept once.
- Importing exchanges: paste a text (an email with its headers is read as one), or choose email files (.eml), a JSON list or a CSV file (comma, semicolon or tab). A preview shows what was understood before anything is saved: the channel, the direction, the contact (found by the address, then by the name), the date. A row with a problem says which and cannot be chosen; a date that is missing can be typed. What was imported before is not added again. A new setting holds the addresses of the user, to tell a sent email from a received one.
- Classifying: tags, with a colour, on applications, companies, contacts and exchanges (two new models, a polymorphic link); a favourite star on applications, companies and contacts; a priority on applications (none, low, medium, high). A tag is added by its name, with the names that exist offered; two names that differ only by case or accents are one tag. The tags are managed in the options (rename, colour, remove, how many things carry each). The cards show the star, the priority and the tags.
- The board: a search that ignores case and accents and wants every word (in the title, the company, the place, the source, the tags and the people); filters by tag (all of them), favourites, source, company, lowest priority and late reminders; a sort by the order of the board, the last change, the date added, the salary per year in the home currency, the priority, the company or the job title, in the way of the language (accents, numbers), with what cannot be compared last. The view is kept, and the board says how many applications are shown.
- Reading a page: when the popup opens on a job page, it reads the `JobPosting` data the page publishes (title, company, place, contract, remote work, description, salary, day published) and fills the form. Only the page you opened the popup on is read, once, and nothing leaves the browser. The day published follows the time zone of the page when it gives one, and yours when it does not.
